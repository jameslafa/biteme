# Notes

This is a traditional dish from Arles in Provence that requires patience. The aubergines are slowly reduced over several hours until they melt into a thick, flavourful spread. The original recipe suggests cooking it in stages over two days for the best consistency, but you can achieve good results in one long session. Use a very low heat throughout to avoid burning. Serve hot over rice, as a pasta sauce, or cold on toasted bread. If serving with rice, remember to put it on about twenty minutes before the riste is done. The dish freezes well and can be easily extended with extra water and flour if you need to serve more people.

# Ingredients

- 5 medium [aubergines]
- [Olive oil], as needed
- 2 medium [onions]
- 2 tins (400 g) [chopped tomatoes]
- 1 clove [garlic]
- 1 sprig [thyme]
- 1 [bay leaf]
- 1 tsp [sugar]
- [Water], as needed
- [Herbes de Provence], to taste

# Instructions

1. Remove the tough stem ends from {aubergine}. Cut them in half or quarters lengthwise depending on size, then slice into pieces one to two centimetres thick. The pieces should be fairly large but easy to stir.
2. Heat a little {olive oil} in a large pot over medium-low heat. Add {aubergine} and cook uncovered, stirring frequently, until they have reduced by about two-thirds and lost most of their water. This takes about fifteen minutes. Don't let them burn. Set aside in a bowl.
3. If there is clean {olive oil} at the bottom of the aubergine bowl, pour it back into the pot. Otherwise add a little fresh oil. Add {onion} and cook over low heat until golden and translucent, about five to seven minutes. Do not let them brown or burn.
4. Add {chopped tomatoes}, {sugar}, and crushed {garlic}. Cook for about five minutes, stirring occasionally, to cook off the raw tinned taste.
5. Return the cooked {aubergine} to the pot. Add {thyme}, {bay leaf}, and a pinch of {herbes de Provence}. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
6. Reduce the heat to very low. Half-cover the pot and simmer very gently, stirring regularly and adding splashes of {water} whenever the mixture looks dry. Continue for two to three hours. An electric hob works better than gas for this, as gas is too fierce even at minimum.
7. When {aubergine} starts to break down and melt, stir well to break up any large pieces. Continue cooking for another one to two hours, stirring often, until the mixture is thick and jammy. If serving with rice, put it on about twenty minutes before the end.
8. Taste and adjust seasoning. Remove {thyme} sprig and {bay leaf} before serving.

# Serving Suggestions

Serve hot over rice, toss with pasta as a sauce, or spread cold on toasted bread as an appetiser. The riste can be refrigerated and reheated, and actually improves with a second gentle reheating the next day.
